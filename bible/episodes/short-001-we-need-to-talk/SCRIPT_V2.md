- **Channel:** Oddly Relatable · **Format:** native 9:16 short (Format A, T1) · **Length:** about 50 s · 1080×1920 · 30 fps
- **Characters:**
  - **HIM:** Canonical Character 01, on screen. A decent, slightly guilty man who overthinks.
  - **HER:** his wife, **off screen**, present only as her messages (contact label **"Wife ❤️"**). Calm, short replies, unintentionally terrifying.
- **Setting:** the canonical living room, evening.
- **How the conversation is shown:** a text-message exchange. HER messages appear as left-side bubbles, HIS as right-side bubbles, stacked in the upper part of the frame (clear of the platform buttons at the bottom), with his reaction pose below. A "typing…" indicator carries the silences. No voices; optional sound: message ding, typing tick, one music sting at the end.
- **Camera:** wide and medium only, zoom ≤ 1.5×.
- **One-line pitch:** he confesses to every crime except the one he's about to be sentenced for.

**Ending:** the callback does two things. He would rather talk about his own crimes than a month with her mother. Then his escape route backfires, because she never knew about the dishwasher. The comedy comes from him, so it stays warm and the wife is never the villain.

## Why it fits the direction
- **A real two-person conversation:** she sets the tension, and he keeps making it worse.
- **Recognisable beats:** the unexplained "We need to talk", typing dots that vanish, over-explaining, confessing to things nobody asked about, "Define March", and a short reply with no question mark.
- **Misunderstandings:** she doesn't know about the dishwasher, and he thinks it's about him when it's about her mother.
- **Punchline plus a button:** the callback gets the laugh, then her final line turns the tables.
