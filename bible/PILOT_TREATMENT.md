## 1. Premise

Femi, at work, receives a text from his wife Amara: **"We need to talk. Tonight."** With no context, no emoji and no kiss, his imagination goes straight to catastrophe. Over one workday he prepares, confesses, bargains and panics. When evening comes and Amara finally speaks, the "serious conversation" is about something completely different. And then Femi's phone buzzes.

**One-line pitch:** *A man who prepares for every disaster except the actual one.*

**Theme:** a short message with no tone is an invitation to imagine the worst, and people who love each other often fear the wrong thing. (Warm, never mean: both of them contributed to the mess.)

## 4. Story outline

### Beginning: the text (0:00–0:35)
- **Hook (0:00–0:08):** a phone on a desk. A message ping. The Natter bubble: **"We need to talk. Tonight."** Femi's brows lift off his head.
- **Setup:** Femi's normal day at his desk. Dez walks by with a cheery line (optional). Femi replies "Sure! 🙂" then "Everything okay?" then deletes it. No answer comes: the *typing dots* appear and vanish.
- **Planted items:** Amara's ring binder and sticky notes (shown on the way out of the house in a one-shot flashback), Femi's drone box hidden in the flat, the "It's fine" counter, Pepper on the sofa, and Femi's charger drawer. These pay off later.

### Middle: the spiral (0:35–2:00)
Each step is caused by the last:

1. **Worst-Case Theatre #1 (grey imagination tint):** Amara packing suitcases; a lawyer; Pepper being split in a custody hearing. Quick, absurd.
2. **Femi calls Tunde** from the stairwell or visits the kiosk. Tunde's confident, invented-proverb advice: *"When a woman says talk, you talk first."* Femi decides to **confess everything before she can**, preparing a list.
3. **Femi's pre-emptive confession list** (on a ridiculous scroll of paper): the drone, the leftovers he ate, the time he pretended to like her cousin's cooking, the charger he "lost". The list grows until it reaches the floor. *(Scale cheat.)*
4. **Madam F's cameo (misdirection seed):** she appears at his desk: "Femi, a quick question." Doom Tint. It turns out to be about a stapler. Relief, then his phone pings again: Amara: "Dinner is ready. Don't be late." *(Femi reads this as ominous.)*
5. **He buys an enormous bouquet** (comedically oversized) and sprints home, drawing stares on the bus. A short premium-motion candidate (see §7).

### Misdirection: the confession (2:00–2:20)
- **Living room, evening.** Femi enters with flowers behind his back. Amara sits with the ring binder and her **reading glasses** (the serious-talk sign). Doom Tint. Pepper stares.
- Before she can speak, Femi launches into the confession: the drone, the leftovers, the cousin's cooking.
- **Amara's reactions escalate** from confused to amused ("What drone?"), so the audience realises *she* does not know about the drone. Femi has just caused his own problem.
- **Misdirection:** the audience, like Femi, now expects the big "talk" to be about trust or the future.

### Ending: the reveal and the final twist (2:20–2:45)
- **The reveal:** Amara opens the binder: **"My mother is coming. For three months."** The binder contains the schedule (Exhibit A: the kitchen takeover; Exhibit B: the cooler).
- **Balance beat (important):** Amara admits *she* is scared to tell him and she has been avoiding it for a week because she cannot say no to her mother. The ominous text was her clumsy attempt to ask for help. They are on the same side. She also discovers the drone; Femi gets a small comeuppance ("We will discuss the drone"), but she is smiling.
- **Sweet moment:** she reveals the sticky notes she'd hidden in his bag all week. (Humanising callback.)
- **Final twist (2:35–2:45):** the doorbell rings. **Mummy Eze stands there, a day early, with a cooler**: "Balogun!" Femi and Amara freeze together, brows lifting off in unison.
- **Closing beat:** Femi's phone pings. **Madam F: "Femi, we need to talk. Monday, 9 a.m."** Doom Tint drops. Pepper blinks slowly. Hold. Sonic logo.

> **Intended reading:** the title's promise ("When Your Wife Says We Need to Talk") is paid off by a wife who needed to talk about something unthreatening, and then the title's phrase returns, unexpectedly, from the boss.

## 5. Main comedic conflict and escalation

- **Core conflict:** Femi's catastrophic imagination vs Amara's plain intention.
- **Escalation ladder:** ambiguous text → worst-case imagining → bad advice → oversized confession list → a "quick question" scare → oversized bouquet → full confession on the sofa.
- **Misdirection:** every step makes the audience believe something serious is coming.
- **Punchline:** the reason for the talk is a harmless (and relatable) family visit.
- **Final twist:** Mummy arrives early; then the boss uses the same phrase.

## 6. Why the balance rule holds [PROPOSED]

| Femi's fault | Amara's fault |
|---|---|
| He assumes the worst and over-confesses | She sent a cold text and delayed telling him for a week |
| He hides gadget purchases | She cannot say no to her mother |
| He avoids asking a simple question | She uses "loaded" scheduling (reading glasses and a binder) that scares him |

Both are likeable; neither is the villain. They finish on the same side, facing Mummy together.

## 10. Production notes and risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Too much dialogue for 2:45 | Trim the confession list to three items on screen; the scroll gag does the rest visually |
| Femi appears like a fool | Give him the best lines in the Tunde call; Amara admits her share; he gets one moment of genuine tenderness |
| "Mummy" jokes feel cliché | Mummy is warm and a competent organiser; she is never mocked for her culture |
| Twist feels unfair | Plant: the ring binder on the way out, Amara's phone call "I'll tell him tonight", Pepper next to a travel bag (a *misleading* clue that actually is Mummy's cooler) |
| Doom Tint overused | Maximum four uses, each with a different trigger |
| Cost overrun | Pilot can be done entirely with Tier 1–2 methods; premium clips optional |
