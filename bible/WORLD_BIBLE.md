## 1. The city [PROPOSED, D6]

An **unnamed, modern West African city**: busy, warm, a little chaotic, full of ambition. It is never named, so a viewer in Manchester, Toronto or Nairobi thinks "a big city like mine" while Nigerian viewers recognise the details.

**Local details shown without explanation** (understood from context and visuals):
- Power cuts: the room goes dark, a generator hums, everyone carries a phone torch.
- Yellow shared buses and keke tricycles, drawn simply, never as stereotypes.
- Jollof, suya, plantain and meat pies as food props.
- Street vendors, a phone-repair kiosk, a church on one corner and a mosque on another, shown without jokes about either.
- Polite greeting culture, big family events, and mothers arriving with food.

**Rule of thumb:** if a local detail needs a footnote, show it with a visual and let the character's reaction explain it. A joke must still land for a viewer who has never been to the city.

**Fictional names used:** Brightline Logistics (office), FixAm Phones (Tunde's kiosk), Natter (messaging app), Fresh Mart (supermarket), Café Nimbus (café), Lagoon Grill (restaurant). All invented. No real brands.

## 2. Reuse system [PROPOSED]

Production-cost rules that shape every set:

1. **One set = one plate family.** Each location has 2–4 fixed camera angles ("plates"), drawn once at 3840×2160 and reused across all episodes.
2. **Three depth layers** per plate (back, mid, optional foreground) so pans and zooms give parallax from still images.
3. **Props are separate cut-outs** that can be added, moved or hidden, so a plate can be re-dressed without redrawing it.
4. **Day/night and mood variants** come from tints and a small number of lighting swap layers, not new art.
5. **Floor line fixed at 72% of frame height** in medium shots so every puppet stands on the same ground.
6. **Total launch kit:** 7 locations × about 3 plates ≈ 20 plates. That covers months of episodes.
