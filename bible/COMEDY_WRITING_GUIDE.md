# Comedy and Storytelling Guide

> ⚠️ **PARTLY SUPERSEDED (owner direction, 2026-11-11):** the channel is for the **world at large**: **no Nigerian/African-specific stories, settings, names or language, and Femi is retired.** The cast, names, locations, language notes and jokes in this document were drafted for a West African setting and are **paused pending a rewrite**. Treat them as archive and templates (roles, structure and technique may carry over), **not as approved content**. See `DECISIONS.md` (AP-16 to AP-18).

**Status:** the six-beat structure is the framework you supplied **[APPROVED as a starting structure]**. Everything else is **[PROPOSED]**.
**Approved constraints:** humour should feel natural, not forced; avoid over-explaining; let expressions, timing and reactions carry the joke. [APPROVED]

## 1. The six-beat structure [APPROVED starting structure]

| # | Beat | Job | Rough share of runtime |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** | Capture attention in the first 3–5 seconds | 5% |
| 2 | **Setup** | Establish who, where and what is at stake | 15–20% |
| 3 | **Escalation** | Make it more ridiculous, in a chain where each step causes the next | 35–40% |
| 4 | **Misdirection** | Lead the audience to a wrong interpretation | 10–15% |
| 5 | **Punchline** | Deliver the real payoff and break the misdirection | 10% |
| 6 | **Closing beat** | A final small joke, callback or image that sticks | 5–8% |

### How each beat is written [PROPOSED]

**Hook.** Open on the funniest *situation*, not on explanation. Start in motion: a text arrives, a door opens, a face freezes. The first line is dialogue or a visual, never a greeting. Test: could someone mute the video and still get curious?

**Setup.** Establish three facts only: *who wants what, what is in the way, what happens if it goes wrong.* Plant at least two items that will pay off later (a prop, a habit, a line). If setup runs long, cut details, not stakes.

**Escalation.** Each step must be *caused by* the last one ("and therefore", not "and then"). Raise one thing per step: stakes, embarrassment, number of people, speed or absurdity. Three to five steps. The third is usually the biggest laugh; do not peak early.

**Misdirection.** The audience should follow the characters' wrong theory with the characters. Plant honest, ambiguous information so the twist feels fair. Never lie to the audience; let characters be wrong while the audience stays "in the know" just enough to enjoy it.

**Punchline.** The reveal reframes what we saw. It should be short: one line or one image. Cut quickly. Over-explaining kills it (see §7).

**Closing beat.** A tag: a callback, a second small twist or a silent reaction (Pepper's stare, the Doom Tint). Aim for a smile, not another big laugh. End on a held image or line, not a recap.

## 2. Character-driven humour [PROPOSED]

- **Jokes come from who is speaking.** The same situation produces different jokes from Femi (catastrophe), Amara (receipts), Tunde (terrible confidence), Dez (hustle jargon), Madam F (polite menace) and Zee (live commentary).
- **Each character has a "comedy engine":**

| Character | Engine |
|---|---|
| Femi | Anxiety about an imagined disaster |
| Amara | Directness that exposes the truth in the worst moment |
| Tunde | Confidence without competence |
| Dez | Buzzwords covering emptiness |
| Madam F | Calm that terrifies |
| Zee | Gossip and live narration |
| Mummy Eze | Love as an operation |
| Pepper | Silent judgement |

- **Want vs. obstacle.** Every scene: the character wants something concrete; something in the way is funny.
- **Flaws drive plot.** Femi avoids conflict, so a small issue becomes a crisis. Amara cannot say no to her mother, so Mummy arrives. Let flaws, not coincidences, create trouble.
- **Warmth underneath.** Even in a fight they are on each other's side. Audiences laugh more when they like the people.

## 3. Dialogue style [PROPOSED, D7]

- **Short lines.** Aim for 4–12 words. Longer only for rambling (Femi nervous, Zee's voice note, Mummy's principal speeches), and then the length is the joke.
- **Subtext over text.** "It's fine" means it is not. "We need to talk" means anything.
- **Interruptions and overlaps** where natural. Use sparingly: they are the main source of rhythm.
- **Language mix:** clear international English as the base; light Nigerian English and occasional Pidgin ("No wahala", "Wetin happen?", "I'm coming") where context makes the meaning obvious. Captions are always on so everyone can follow. Never write dialect phonetically in a way that mocks.
- **Specifics beat generics.** "Four per cent battery and the charger is in the drawer of doom" beats "he is disorganised".
- **Read it aloud.** If a line cannot be spoken in one breath at normal pace, cut it.
- **Word budget:** about 130–150 spoken words per minute including pauses (comedy has air). A 2:45 episode is about 330–400 words.

## 4. Physical and visual comedy [PROPOSED]

Because the default production is pose-based, physical comedy is built from **poses, cuts and timing**, not fluid motion:

- **Pose snap:** instant change to an exaggerated pose on a sound hit (e.g. frozen vertical posture).
- **Smash cut** between confident and ruined.
- **Scale cheats:** the bill or text message grows.
- **Brow lift-off, sweat escalation, Doom Tint drop** (see `VISUAL_IDENTITY.md` §13).
- **Shake and bounce keyframes** for shock; **slide-ins** for entrances; **door slams** on a hit.
- **Impact frames**: a single stylised frame on the hit, then back.
- Anything needing true limb motion (tumbling, a chase, a slapstick chain) is a candidate for premium motion (`ANIMATION_DIRECTION.md` §8).

## 5. Situational comedy and the "ordinary made absurd" rule [PROPOSED]

Start from a situation almost everyone has lived: the ominous text, the group chat, the bill, the meeting, the in-laws. Push it **one logical step further** each beat. The audience laughs because they recognise it and because it is slightly more extreme than they dared.

Formula: **[Relatable trigger] + [Character flaw] + [Escalating consequence].**

## 6. Sarcasm and exaggeration [PROPOSED]

- Sarcasm belongs mostly to **Amara (dry)**, **Zee (loud)** and **Madam F (polite)**. Each has a distinct flavour; do not blend them.
- Exaggeration must stay **internally consistent**: an exaggeration is funniest when characters treat it as normal.
- Sarcasm punches *at behaviour*, not at identity.
- If a sarcastic line needs a reaction to land, give it one: an eyebrow, a pause, Pepper's stare.

## 7. Don't over-explain [APPROVED principle; methods PROPOSED]

1. **Cut the explanation line.** If the audience has just seen the joke, do not have a character say what it means.
2. **Show the reaction, not the label.** A held look is funnier than "I can't believe it".
3. **Trust a pause.** One to two seconds of silence often beats an extra line.
4. **Let one thing be implied.** The audience enjoys completing the joke.
5. **Narration never explains a joke.** Narrators supply facts; characters supply laughs.
6. **Final check:** read the script and delete every line that restates something the picture already shows.

## 8. Pacing and comedic pauses [PROPOSED]

| Moment | Timing guide |
|---|---|
| Hook | First laugh or strong curiosity within 5 s |
| Standard exchange | 2–4 s per line including reaction |
| Pause before a punchline | 0.4–0.8 s (the "beat") |
| Awkward silence | 1.0–2.0 s, held on faces |
| After the biggest laugh | Let it breathe for 0.5–1.0 s before the next line |
| Reaction cut-in | 0.5–1.0 s |
| Montage or rapid-fire sequences | 0.3–0.6 s per cut |
| Closing beat | Hold 1.5–2.5 s |

Edit rule: the cut goes **just after** the line lands, never during the laugh.
Pose-based animation relies on timing even more than motion does. A frame held 6 frames too long kills a joke.

## 9. Cultural humour rules [PROPOSED, D20]

1. **Warm, never mocking.** The jokes are about situations and characters, not about being Nigerian, Igbo, Yoruba, Ghanaian, Muslim, Christian or any group.
2. **In-law humour is allowed with respect.** Mummy Eze is overbearing and loving, never evil, never stupid. The family always wins together.
3. **Explain by showing.** Pidgin and local words must be understandable from context; captions help.
4. **Avoid stereotypes** (scammers, corruption jokes, tribal jokes, poverty as punchline).
5. **Money jokes target situations** (the bill, price hikes, "borrow me small") not poverty.
6. **No religion or politics as punchlines.** Church or mosque can appear as setting.
7. **Sensitivity pass** before locking every script: who might be hurt, and is the joke worth it?

## 10. Running jokes [PROPOSED]

A running joke is a *promise*: the audience expects it, and the pleasure is in the variation.

**Rules**
- Maximum **two** running gags per episode.
- A gag must change each time (new setting, bigger scale, subverted expectation, or paid off with a twist).
- Introduce it in the setup, escalate it, then pay it off or flip it in the closing beat.
- Retire a gag when its variations run out.

**Starter running-gag list**

| Gag | Owner | Variation ideas |
|---|---|---|
| "It's fine" tally counter | Femi | The counter rolls over; the counter says "fine" to him |
| Ring binder exhibits | Amara | Exhibit grows; Femi creates his own binder |
| Charger drawer | Femi | Drawer of doom opens to reveal something unexpected |
| "As my grandfather said…" | Tunde | Mummy replies with a real proverb that crushes it |
| Side-hustle counter | Dez | The counter hits 10; one actually works |
| "Quick question" Doom Tint | Madam F | The question is genuinely quick (and that is scarier) |
| Voice-note duration bar | Zee | 47 minutes; a voice note about a voice note |
| Pepper's stare | Pepper | Pepper finally does something |
| Mummy's cooler | Mummy | A second, bigger cooler |
| Broken lift sign | Office | The lift works once, and nobody trusts it |

## 11. Unexpected twists [PROPOSED]

- **The innocent reveal:** the scary thing was harmless (the pilot).
- **The reversal:** the expected victim wins.
- **The callback twist:** the planted prop pays off.
- **The identity twist:** the wrong person was being talked about.
- **The sincerity twist:** a joke episode ends with an unexpectedly sweet beat.
- **The escalation twist:** the fix is worse than the problem.

**Twist test:** after the reveal, the audience should think *"of course"* and not *"that came from nowhere"*. Re-read the earlier beats: is the clue visible?

## 12. Rules for avoiding repetitive jokes [PROPOSED]

1. **Keep a Joke Ledger** (one line per joke: episode, setting, mechanism). Review before writing every script.
2. **Vary the mechanism**, not just the topic. Mechanisms include: misunderstanding, exaggeration, reversal, callback, sarcasm, physical pose gag, scale cheat, silent reaction, wordplay, rule-of-three.
3. **No more than one use of the same mechanism per episode in the same beat position.**
4. **Rule of three, then break it.** Two repeats plus a violation.
5. **Rotate the "fool".** Track who is wrong in each episode (see `CHARACTER_BIBLE.md` §11).
6. **Rotate endings.** Do not end three episodes in a row on a Pepper stare or a Doom Tint.
7. **Fresh pairings.** Not every episode uses Femi + Amara; give Amara + Zee, Femi + Dez, Tunde + Zee, Madam F + Dez their own outings.
8. **Retire tired formulas.** If you can guess the punchline from the title, change the title or the punchline.
9. **Every five episodes:** a review of the ledger and balance log; retire or refresh anything that is running dry.

## 13. Script quality checklist [PROPOSED]

Before a script is locked, confirm:
- [ ] The hook works with the sound off.
- [ ] Setup contains no unneeded details.
- [ ] Each escalation step causes the next.
- [ ] The twist is fair (clue visible earlier).
- [ ] No line explains a joke the picture already shows.
- [ ] Balance log updated; no character is always the fool.
- [ ] Joke Ledger checked; no repeated mechanism.
- [ ] Cultural sensitivity pass done.
- [ ] Every line can be read aloud in one breath.
- [ ] Runtime estimate within the format target.
- [ ] Reaction beats and pauses are written into the script.
